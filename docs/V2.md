# v2: a deeper prior and adaptive interval width

Same data, splits and metrics as above. `python -m evprior evaluate` and `ved_eval` now also run the regimes `full_cat` and `no_history_cat`;
`experiments/compare_v2.py` and `compare_ved.py` write `results/v2_compare*.json`; the model search is in `experiments/prior_search*.py`.

**New prior `physics_cat`.** The physics ridge is kept; the residual is learned by CatBoost (depth 8, 1000 trees, learning rate 0.04) instead of XGBoost (depth 4, 400 trees).
Depth was the lever: on grouped CV (segment-level models, trip WAPE) XGBoost depth 4 / 6 gave 6.56% / 6.40% and CatBoost depth 4 / 6 / 8 gave 6.53% / 6.42% / 6.23%. Compared on every evaluation scheme:

| trip-energy WAPE | v1 `physics_gbm` | v2 `physics_cat` |
|---|---|---|
| grouped 5-fold CV | 6.68% | **6.16%** |
| forward chaining by month | 8.70% | **7.56%** |
| leave one vehicle out | 10.56% | **10.41%** |
| season out (winter / non-winter) | 11.10% | **9.25%** |
| Nissan Leafs, nothing refit (no route history) | 66.98% | **47.55%** |

Remaining-energy error at 10 km driven (relative to the energy still to be used):

| | grouped CV | forward chaining | leave one vehicle out | season out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| prior alone, v1 / v2 | 6.20% / **5.73%** | 8.39% / **7.19%** | 9.45% / **9.31%** | 11.64% / **9.35%** |
| Kalman with vehicle carry-over, v1 / v2 | 6.20% / **5.82%** | 7.39% / **6.45%** | **8.13%** / 8.27% | 7.76% / **7.60%** |

On the Nissan Leafs at 5 km driven the error is 36.7% to **27.5%** (prior alone), 29.7% to **22.8%** (Kalman multiplier) and 22.8% to **21.0%** (with carry-over).
Two limits: with the new prior the online update adds nothing in distribution (5.73% prior alone against 5.82% with carry-over), as before; and for an unseen vehicle the gain of the better prior is
absorbed by the carry-over (8.13% against 8.27%, i.e. not better). The Leafs are still over-predicted by 44% in total trip energy.

**Adaptive interval width (`adaptive.py`).** The stored 90% intervals keep their shape; their width is multiplied by k, updated after every finished trip with the adaptive conformal
inference rule (log k += 0.05 x (miss rate - 0.10)), trips in order of start time. Coverage of the 90% interval for `kf_carry` at 5 km or more, fixed width against adaptive:

| scheme | v1 prior | v2 prior |
|---|---|---|
| grouped CV | 90.4% to 89.6% | 90.1% to 89.2% (width -3%) |
| forward chaining | 88.3% to 88.4% | 91.3% to 90.7% |
| leave one vehicle out | 83.3% to **88.1%** (width +18%) | 85.2% to **89.5%** (+15%) |
| season out | 86.6% to **90.1%** (+8%) | 90.7% to 91.5% (+2%) |

The adaptive width repairs the two shifted schemes for either prior and costs nothing when the intervals were already right. The gamma of 0.05 was fixed in advance;
the other values tried (0.02, 0.1, 0.2) give coverage within 1.5 points of it.

**Tried and rejected (reported so they are not tried again):**
- **Multi-component filter** (`kalman_multi.py`, a state per physics component: propulsion with the learned residual, and heating, plus an additive term; it reduces exactly to the original filter with one component, tested). Remaining-energy error at 10 km, multi-component against the original full filter: grouped CV 6.11% against 6.10%, leave one vehicle out 8.71% against 8.64%, season out 10.72% against 10.71%. No gain on any scheme, so the single multiplier stays.
- **Route-history features** (leave-own-trip-out averages of speed spread, stop share, energy per km and power in the 500 m cell, 6 variants): 6.23% to 6.19% to 6.29%, within noise. The speed-history group of v1 already carries what the model can use.
- **Averaging CatBoost, physics_cat (and XGBoost)**: no consistent gain over physics_cat alone (two-model average 6.15%, 7.64%, 10.39%, 10.11% on the four schemes against 6.16%, 7.56%, 10.41%, 9.25%).
